Things to do in Castel Gandolfo

A guide written by local hosts: the sights, the lake and the surroundings, with official links for opening times and tickets.

In town

Papal Palace and gardens

Commissioned by Pope Urban VIII and designed by Carlo Maderno, the Papal Palace was the popes’ summer residence for centuries. Since 2016 it has been a museum: you can visit the papal apartment, the historical collections and the gardens, with terraces overlooking the lake.

When the Pope stays in Castel Gandolfo some rooms may be closed, so check the calendar before you go.

The Vatican Observatory domes

On the roof of the Papal Palace stand the two domes of the Pope’s astronomical observatory, with historic telescopes and a panoramic terrace over the lake. Visits are guided, in small groups, and last about 45 minutes.

Access is by a spiral staircase, so the visit is not suitable for strollers or wheelchairs.

Book the visit

Piazza della Libertà and San Tommaso

The heart of the town. The square is framed by the Papal Palace and the Collegiate Church of San Tommaso da Villanova, designed by Gian Lorenzo Bernini (1658–1661). In the middle stands the so-called Bernini fountain: the design is almost certainly by Carlo Maderno, and Bernini moved it in 1660.

Between the town hall and the post office is what is remembered as the world’s first public postbox, dating from 1820.

The fountain

The old town and its viewpoints

Lanes, small shops and views over the crater: from the old town you can see the lake and, across it, Monte Cavo. The railway station sits just below the town, high above the lake.

The lake

Lake Albano

An ancient volcanic crater about 300 metres above sea level, where the rowing and canoe races of the 1960 Rome Olympics were held. From the town you can walk down to the lakeside for a stroll along the shore or lunch with a view.

Lake Albano on Wikipedia

Kayaks, canoes and pedal boats

Pedal boats, canoes and small sailing boats leave from the wooden jetties on the lakeside; there are no ferry services. If you would like a guide, there are organised kayak tours.

Kayak tour

Along the shore: nymphaeum and emissary

On the western shore is the Ninfeo Bergantino, a grotto 17 metres across that was part of Domitian’s villa. Further along is the Emissary, a Roman tunnel 1,800 metres long dug around 398 BC to control the level of the lake.

Around the area

Nemi and its lake

A few kilometres away lies Lake Nemi, smaller and more secluded, with the village famous for its wild strawberries. The Museum of the Roman Ships is closed for works until 31 March 2027.

Museum of the Roman Ships

Castelli Romani Regional Park

Woods and trails above the two lakes, all the way up to Monte Cavo: walks for a morning or a whole day.

The park on Turismo Roma

Ariccia, Albano and Frascati

The Castelli Romani make an easy day out: Ariccia for porchetta and Palazzo Chigi, then Albano, and Frascati with its villas.

Rome for the day

From Castel Gandolfo station the FL4 regional train runs to Roma Termini. For the Vatican Museums and the Colosseum, book ahead and only on the official websites.
